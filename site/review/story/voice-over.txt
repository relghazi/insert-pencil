INTRO COMIC: VOICE-OVER
(Spoken in the game by her, a few years older, as each page opens. Never lettered.)

PAGE 1 (class, one life left on her doodle)
Everybody has one class where their pencil starts drawing on its own. Mine was math. While the board filled up with numbers, my notebook filled up with ships, saucers and little people to save.

PAGE 2 (the bell, the flyer: the Quarter Moon is closing)
Then I saw the flyer. The Quarter Moon, the tiny arcade on our street, was closing on Saturday. And on the chalkboard behind the counter, every single line said the same three letters.

PAGE 3 (the coin jar, seventy-five cents, three days)
My friends did the math faster than I did. Beating him would take about a hundred and sixty tries. I had seventy-five cents, a jar, and three days.

PAGE 4 (mowing lawns, chores, every quarter counts)
So I worked. Lawns, dishes, a dog that hated me. Every quarter went in the jar, and every clink sounded a little like a chance.

PAGE 5 (practising on paper)
Quarters were for the real thing. Paper was free. I drew the whole game in my notebook and played it with a pencil, wave after wave, until I knew where everything would come from.

PAGE 6 (Saturday, the rain, the closing sign)
Saturday came with rain. I tied the jar to the back of my bike, lid screwed on tight, and pedaled the whole way.

PAGE 7 (she made it; the old man behind the counter)
The old man behind the counter just smiled, like he had been waiting for me.

PAGE 8 (the run: last quarter, one life left, new high score)
I don't remember breathing. I remember the last quarter, the last life, and my friends going completely silent behind me. And then the number turned over.

PAGE 9 (the storm, the blackout, the pencil)
The storm took the lights a second later. In the dark he showed me the pencil he had used for forty years of high scores. He said arcades don't close. They move.

PAGE 10 (two weeks later: her initials in the ledger)
Two weeks later, I wrote it in his ledger myself, in my own handwriting. Four one two, three nine oh.

PAGE 11 (Monday at her locker, the No. 2 Arcade notebook)
On Monday I opened my locker and started a new notebook. Twenty-four games, all of them mine now. Your turn.
