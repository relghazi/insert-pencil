Hi Dave and Dan, and thank you both for helping!

The plan: first find a better story (Dave), then redo the comic in one consistent style (Dan). The story is open, so please treat everything here as a starting point rather than something to preserve.

No. 2 Arcade is a free browser game: 24 classic arcade games from 1972 to 1985, each remade as one of a high school girl's notebooks, picked from her school locker. Before you reach the locker, an 11-page comic tells the story of how she fell for the little arcade on her street.

A quick honest note: the comic you'll find here was made with an AI image tool and then corrected over many rounds. I like the overall feel, but I think the story can be better, and the characters and the painting drift from page to page, so it doesn't quite read as one book by one hand. That's where you come in.

Good places to start: the 11 lettered pages and the story texts for the current story, and ART-DIRECTION.pdf for the look of the game and the comic, with lots of examples.

The story today

A 15-year-old girl finds out that the Quarter Moon, the tiny arcade on her street, is closing for good on Saturday. Every high score on its chalkboard belongs to ACE, an older boy. Beating his Defender score would take about 160 tries, and she has 75 cents and three days. So she scrapes quarters together, practises on paper, and on Saturday she beats Ace by ten points with her very last quarter. Then a storm knocks the power out while she's typing her initials, and the score is gone. Outside in the rain, the old owner sees her notebook, hands her the pencil he has kept forty years of high scores with, and tells her arcades don't close, they move. Two weeks later, at his new place, he writes her score, RAD 412,390, into his ledger, and Ace says he saw it. The last page finds her at her school locker on Monday, which is where the game itself begins.

The heart of it: a machine forgets, a pencil doesn't.

Story details the game already uses

These appear outside the comic, so a story change may ripple into them (I can redo them, it's just good to know):

  • Her locker is the main menu. The comic currently ends there, on a Monday, with her holding the No. 2 Arcade notebook. The name is a pun on the No. 2 pencil.
  • The cast are in the game: a stack of who's who Polaroids in her locker shows each one with a first name, and on the back a full name and a short note in her handwriting (Ruby Ann Delgado, June Park, Theo Okafor, Adrian "Ace" Ellison, Harold "Hal" Moon, Margaret Albright). Those notes retell the current story (see the cast bios).
  • Each of them made a mixtape that plays in her Walkman: Ruby techno and French house, June new wave, Theo hip hop, Ace heavy metal, Hal jazz ("what played on the counter radio after closing, for forty years"), and a hidden disco tape by Mrs. Albright, unlocked by typing the digits of pi. Ace's songs are about owning the high-score board.
  • Ace is the rival in the game too: every notebook's high-score page shows Ace's score to beat, and beating him in five notebooks (or typing 412390, the score she beat him with) opens Ace's locker, where the hard mode lives.
  • Her first notebook game is Scribble Defender, her version of Defender, the machine she plays in the comic.
  • Her initials are RAD; her winning score is 412,390.
  • The comic is voiced: she narrates each page looking back, a few years older (voice-over.txt), recorded in five languages. A new story means new narration, which is fine.

Things I've settled on

These are choices I've made for the game over time, because the rest of the game is built on them, so it helps to keep them in mind. Everything else is open to your ideas.

The look

  • Fine sepia-navy ink lines with transparent watercolour on warm off-white paper, the paper showing through, soft watercolour blooms. No pure black outlines, glossy digital gradients or photo textures.
  • Page 3 is my favourite page and the one I'd like everything to feel like.
  • When she draws in her notebook, it's in graphite pencil or blue ballpoint, and she draws the characters from her own game (the paper-dart ship, the saucer, the little bug), not generic rockets.
  • The arcade is warm and amber inside, not purple.
  • It's a high school world: muted colours and real printed lettering on signs. I steer away from anything that looks like kindergarten (rainbow, bubble or cut-paper letters) and from anything that looks like Comic Sans.

The characters (see the characters below; the cast bios tell you who they are)

  • Ruby, the hero: 14 to 15, curly dark-blonde hair in a high ponytail with a plain navy hair tie and a few loose curls around her face, blue eyes, and she's left-handed (she draws, writes and holds her pencil with her left hand). Yellow pullover hoodie, blue jeans, white low-profile runners with crossed blue stripes, a thin red stripe, beige suede toe and heel and a cream sole; navy backpack; a wide blue notebook. Her initials are RAD.
  • Yellow is her colour, so nobody else wears or carries yellow (June's boot stitching is the one exception).
  • June, the smallest of the three friends: black bob with bangs, round glasses, olive utility vest, star tee, black jeans, black 8-eyelet boots with yellow stitching.
  • Theo: short locs, a blue and cream varsity jacket with a round blank patch, red and white striped tee, charcoal joggers, white leather shell-toe sneakers with three black stripes. Same build on every page.
  • Ace: the oldest and tallest, but clearly still a teenager. Long black curls, leather jacket, skull tee, black jeans with a worn rip at his right knee, black canvas high-tops with white toe caps, and usually looking bored. He's my own design, so I'd like him not to resemble any actor or TV character.
  • Hal, the owner: late 60s, brown cable-knit cardigan, glasses on a chain, brown loafers, and a chewed, taped pencil behind his right ear until page 8, when he gives it to Ruby.
  • Mrs. Albright, the teacher: purple sweater, hair in a bun. In the comic I only ever see her sleeves, hands or back.
  • Most important of all: each character should look like the same person in every panel.

Names: in the comic itself only ACE is named (on the chalkboard). Elsewhere in the game the cast does have names: the who's who Polaroids in her locker show their first names (Ruby, June, Theo, Ace, Hal, Mrs. Albright), and turned over, their full names and a short note in her handwriting. So please keep names out of the comic panels, but they're yours to use in notes and sheets.

The arcade (see the places below; for whenever the story visits the Quarter Moon)

  • Every machine is a faithful painted version of the real game: cabinet, marquee logo, side art and screen, always switched on and glowing (except in the page 9 blackout).
  • Ruby's machine is a real 1981 Williams DEFENDER, and its screen shows real Defender (the scanner at the top, the hills, landers, little humanoids, the ship and its laser). It always stands in the back-right corner, its back to the right wall, screen facing into the room, with the posters just to its left.
  • Players hold the joystick in their left hand and press the buttons with their right.
  • The high scores are hand-chalked on an old black slate chalkboard behind the counter (black, not green), with the real game names and logos (DEFENDER, ASTEROIDS, PAC-MAN, SPACE INVADERS).
  • The floor plan gives everything a fixed place: windows, door, counter, the gumball machine (on its red stand on the floor, never on the counter) and all eight machines.
  • Ruby's glass coin jar always has a screw-on metal lid (it's open only on page 8, while she takes coins out).

Words

  • I don't use em dashes anywhere in the game.
